04Impact on Project Design

Dr. Parisa Hosseinzadeh

Detect - In an effort to revise our software and gain substantial feedback, we decided to reach out to someone with a considerable amount of experience in computational and synthetic biology. Additionally, our software model is based off of the de novo protein LOCKR, created in the Baker Lab. Being able to reach out to someone who had previous work in the lab and is familiar with the LOCKR system would be able to give us critical and beneficial feedback to ensure our system is optimized.

Research - Dr. Parisa Hosseinzadeh is a computational biochemist and currently a professor in the Department of Bioengineering at the University. She trained as a molecular biologist in her undergraduate studies, and previously worked as a postdoctoral fellow at David Baker’s Lab at the Institute for Protein Design, located in the University of Washington. Presently, she runs her own lab, called the Hosseinzadeh Lab, and primarily focuses on the design of novel peptides and proteins, with the combination of computational and experimental approaches. She has completed significant work and published numerous papers in protein and peptide design. We asked her questions such as “Do you believe our usage of the LOCKR de novo system is a novel approach to this issue?” and “Are there any issues or implications of the implementation of LOCKR that you foresee?” to further optimize our software.

Outcome - After conversing with Dr. Hosseinzadeh, we furthered our understanding of the development of LOCKR. Dr. Hosseinzadeh provided us with a perspective from working in the Baker Lab, and told us about her experience. One specific piece of advice Dr. Hosseinzadeh provided us with was to replace alanine less in our software LATTE. She advised us that replacing alanine in a couple places is great to reduce acidity, but replacing it in multiple places can affect the way the binder itself works, and can be problematic and may not even work at all without alanine. Instead, she told us to ensure that once you know which part of the LOCKR is interacting with the key, make sure to keep that part itself constant, and to design the peptide normally and then insert it into our software.

Pursue - As a result of our meeting with Dr. Hosseinzadeh, we utilized her feedback on the use of alanines and ensured to keep the areas of LOCKR that interact with the key constant. Dr. Hosseinzadeh also led us to choosing specific positions to preserve rather than every D or glutamic or aspartic acid switches to alanine. Additionally, she provided us with contacts to Dr. Scott Boyken and Dr. Robert Langan, two main researchers helping develop LOCKR in the baker lab, helping us further our feedback received on our software.
