Overview

Our wet-lab works to test whether LATTE’s charge-optimization system meaningfully improves the function of our de-novo derived PfLDH-binding protein when it is integrated as LOCKR’s sensing domain in the LOCKR switch. Our de-novo binders were derived from a pipeline of RFDiffusion, ProteinMPNN, and AlphaFold, and we tested for target affinity using our own software, HybriDock-Pe. Then, LATTE runs a liability and charge scan to modify this binder, creating a charge-optimized version that reduces the electrostatic interference with cage-key thermodynamics.

We are expressing three different constructs which isolate the effect of the charge-optimization and the tandem avidity, which is the combined binding strength of the two tandem binders. Our constructs include a cage with the original binder, a cage with the charge-optimized binder, and a tandem cage with two charge-optimized binders that are linked by a glycerin linker. The first pair isolates the effect of charge optimization while the second pair isolates the effect of tandem avidity. Here, we are using malaria as our proof-of-concept validator, with PfLDH, the malarial target and binders optimized for that target specifically.

Before testing our constructs, we will express the published LOCKR system ourselves, with IgG1-Fc as our target, to ensure that our workflow of expression, purification, and luminescence measurement is successful. Thus, we can attribute the differences between the experiments to the shifting experimental variables rather than a mishap in our experimental design.

02Materials and Methods

Bacterial Strains

Plasmid transformations were done with the use of BL21-DE3(F– ompT hsdSB (rB–, mB–) gal dcm (DE3)) cells, using these cells to allow for high levels of recombinant protein expression driven by the T7 promoter found in the genome. The cage component, contains a kanamycin resistance, and is is encoded on the pET28a vector (ColE1-type origin), while the Key component, contains a chloramphenicol, is encoded on the pACYCDuet vector (p15A origin). The compatibility between these two vectors allows for co-expression of both proteins within one cell.
